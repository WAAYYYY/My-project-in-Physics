Visualisation of some vectors fields in a Cylinder

By Adrien Semik Le Du Da Silva
03/07/2026

Introduction and motivation

Now in this section we are going to visualize the vector field in a Cylinder and introduce the concept of Riemannian metric and connexion. So if you want a brief introduction about vector fields and integral curves, you can check the previous section about the vector field in a saddle surface. Let's build the surface of a cylinder in \(\mathbb{R}^3\) defined by the equation : $$ \begin{equation} \phi(\theta, z) = (R \cos(\theta), R \sin(\theta), z) \end{equation} $$ where \(R\) is the radius of the cylinder, \(\theta\) is the angle around the cylinder, and \(z\) is the height along the cylinder's axis. The tangent space at each point on the cylinder is spanned by the vectors \(\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial \theta}\) and \(\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial z}\). Let's introduce what is the Riemannian metric and the connexion on the cylinder. The Riemannian metric is a way to measure distances and angles on the surface of the cylinder. It is defined by the inner product of tangent vectors at each point on the surface. first we calculate the tangent vectors: $$ \begin{equation} \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial \theta} = (-R \sin(\theta), R \cos(\theta), 0), \quad \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial z} = (0, 0, 1) \end{equation} $$ After we do the inner product of these tangent vectors, we can define the Riemannian metric on the cylinder : $$ \begin{equation} g_{\theta\theta} = \left\langle \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial \theta}, \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial \theta} \right\rangle = R^2, \quad g_{\theta z} = \left\langle \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial \theta}, \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial z} \right\rangle = 0, \quad g_{z z} = \left\langle \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial z}, \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial z} \right\rangle = 1 \end{equation} $$ So we can write the metric tensor : $$ \begin{equation} ds^2 = g_{\theta\theta} d\theta^2 + 2 g_{\theta z} d\theta dz + g_{zz} dz^2 = R^2 d\theta^2 + dz^2 \end{equation} $$ Resume in this matrix form, the Riemannian metric on the cylinder is given by : $$ \begin{equation} g_{ij} = \begin{pmatrix} R^2 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \end{equation} $$ This metric allows us to measure lengths and angles on the cylinder. The connection, which defines how to differentiate vector fields along the surface, can be derived from this metric. The connection coefficients (Christoffel symbols) can be computed from the metric tensor, and they describe how the basis vectors change as we move along the surface of the cylinder.
