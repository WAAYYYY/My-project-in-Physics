Visualisation of some vectors fields in a plan on R²

By Adrien Semik Le Du Da Silva
03/07/2026

Introduction and motivation

This page concerne the visualisation of some vector fields in a plan on R². The goal is to provide an intuitive understanding of how vector fields behave in a two-dimensional space. By visualizing these fields, we can gain insights into their properties and applications in physics and engineering. So we are going to create a simple field of vectors in a plan on R². the objectives are to calculate the integral curves of the vector field, to visualize it and to understand the behavior of the vector field in different regions of the plan.
So in this page YOU will be able to choose YOUR vector field in chosing the function $P(x,y)$ and $Q(x,y)$, the initial point $(x_0,y_0)$ and the time interval $[t_0,t_f]$ to visualize the integral curves of the vector field.

Defining a vector field

Let's start by defining a vector field. If we take a subset of $R^n$ we use a vector-valued function $F: U \subset R^n \to R^n$ that assigns a vector to each point in the subset, and admit it smooth (i.e., continuously differentiable). So every smooth vector field $F$ on an open subset $U$ of $R^n$ can be written $\sum_{i=1}^{n} F_i \frac{\partial}{\partial x_i}$, where $F_i$ are the component functions of the vector field. In our case, we will consider a vector field in a plan on $R^2$, we will have a function $F: U \subset R^2 \to R^2$ that assigns a vector to each point in the plan. The vector field can be represented as $F(x,y) = (P(x,y), Q(x,y))$, where $P$ and $Q$ are scalar functions that define the components of the vector at each point $(x,y)$ in the plan.

Integral curves of a vector field

Let's begin simple with a first exemple, and let's take this vector field $$ \begin{equation} X(x,y) = P(x,y) \frac{\partial}{\partial x} + Q(x,y) \frac{\partial}{\partial y} \end{equation} $$ with the dynamical system associated to the vector field $X$ given by the following differential equations: $$ \begin{equation} \frac{dx}{dt} = P(x,y), \quad \frac{dy}{dt} = Q(x,y) \end{equation} $$ Let's take for our irst exemple the vector field $X(x,y) = x \frac{\partial}{\partial x} + -y \frac{\partial}{\partial y}$, so we have $P(x,y) = x$ and $Q(x,y) = -y$. The associated dynamical system is given by the following differential equations: So the curve integral of the vector field $X$ is a curve $\gamma(t) = (x(t), y(t))$ that satisfies the following differential equations: $$ \begin{equation} \frac{dx}{dt} = x, \quad \frac{dy}{dt} = -y \end{equation} $$ The only critical point of the vector field $X$ is the origin $(0,0)$, so the explicite solution of the differential equations is given by the following equations: $$ \begin{equation} x(t) = x_0\exp(t), \quad y(t) = y_0\exp(-t) \end{equation} $$ where $(x_0,y_0)$ is the initial point of the curve integral $\gamma(t)$. We obtain a selle point at the origin, and the integral curves of the vector field $X$ are given by the following equations: $$ \begin{equation} y = \frac{y_0}{x_0} x^{-1} \end{equation} $$ So the integral curves of the vector field $X$ are hyperbolas, and we can visualize them in the plan on $R^2$ by plotting the vector field and the integral curves. The vector field $X$ is represented by arrows that indicate the direction and magnitude of the vector at each point in the plan, and the integral curves are represented by curves that follow the direction of the vector field. The integral curves of the vector field $X$ are hyperbolas that approach the origin as $t \to \infty$ and move away from the origin as $t \to -\infty$.
