The Coherent States of the Harmonic Oscillator

By Adrien Semik Le Du Da Silva
03/07/2026

Introduction

Coherent States of the Quantum Harmonic Oscillator A hands-on exploration of the "most classical" quantum states built directly on the operator algebra from Cohen-Tannoudji's Quantum Mechanics, Vol. 1, Ch. 5. Starting from the eigenvalue equation $a \ket{\alpha} = \alpha \ket{\alpha}$, this piece derives the coherent state decomposition over the number basis, its Poissonian photon-number statistics, and the fact that its expectation values $\left\langle X \right\rangle (t)$ and $\left\langle P \right\rangle (t)$ trace out the exact classical trajectory of the oscillator; all while saturating the Heisenberg uncertainty bound $\Delta X \times \Delta P = \frac{\hbar}{2}$ at every instant. The analytical results are then verified numerically in Python: the wavepacket is built from a superposition of Hermite functions, evolved in time via spectral decomposition, and animated to show it staying perfectly localized in sharp contrast to a generic (non-coherent) Gaussian packet, which visibly spreads out over time.

Analytical construction

A coherent state $\ket{\alpha}$ is defined as the eigenstate of the annihilation operator $a$ with eigenvalue $\alpha \in \mathbb{C}$: We need to construct $\ket{\alpha}$ in the basis of the harmonic oscillator number states $\ket{n}$, which are the eigenstates of the number operator $N = a^\dagger a$ with eigenvalues $n \in \mathbb{N}$.
We define $\ket{\alpha}$ as a linear combination of the number states: $$ \begin{equation} \ket{\alpha} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c_n \ket{n} \end{equation} $$ where $c_n$ are the coefficients to be determined. Applying the annihilation operator $a$ to $\ket{\alpha}$ gives: $$ \begin{equation} a \ket{\alpha} = a \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c_n \ket{n} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c_n a \ket{n} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c_n \sqrt{n} \ket{n-1} = \alpha \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c_n \ket{n} \end{equation} $$ So we have the relation: $$ \begin{equation} \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c_n \sqrt{n} \ket{n-1} = \alpha \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c_n \ket{n} \end{equation} $$ And for $n \geq 1$, we can write: $$ \begin{equation} c_n \sqrt{n} = \alpha c_{n-1} \end{equation} $$ This gives us a recurrence relation for the coefficients $c_n$: $$ \begin{equation} c_n = \frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{n}} c_{n-1} \end{equation} $$ Starting from $c_0$, we can express $c_n$ in terms of $c_0$: $$ \begin{equation} c_n = \frac{\alpha^n}{\sqrt{n!}} c_0 \end{equation} $$ Now we have to deternine $c_0$ by normalizing the state $\ket{\alpha}$, and we write the bra : $$ \begin{equation} \bra{\alpha} = \sum_{m=0}^{\infty} c_m^* \bra{m} \text{ with } c_m^* = \frac{(\alpha^*)^m}{\sqrt{m!}} c_0^* \end{equation} $$ Now let's compute the inner product $\braket{\alpha|\alpha}$: $$ \begin{equation} \braket{\alpha|\alpha} = \sum_{m=0}^{\infty} \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c_m^* c_n \braket{m|n} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} |c_n|^2 \delta_{mn}= |c_0|^2 \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{|\alpha|^{2n}}{n!} = |c_0|^2 e^{|\alpha|^2} \end{equation} $$ We kown that $\braket{\alpha|\alpha} = 1$, so we have: $$ \begin{align} |c_0|^2 e^{|\alpha|^2} &= 1 \\ \Leftrightarrow |c_0|^2 &= e^{-|\alpha|^2} \\ \Leftrightarrow c_0 &= e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}\\ \end{align} $$ So at the end we have the expression of the coherent state $\ket{\alpha}$ in the number basis: $$ \begin{equation} \ket{\alpha} = e^{-|\alpha|^2/2} \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{\alpha^n}{\sqrt{n!}} \ket{n} \end{equation} $$ Now, we would like to compute $\left\langle N \right\rangle$ and $\left\langle N^2 \right\rangle$ to find the variance $\Delta N^2 = \left\langle N^2 \right\rangle - \left\langle N \right\rangle^2$ and show that the photon number statistics of a coherent state is Poissonian. We have:
Let's start with the definition of N : $$ \begin{equation} N = a^\dagger a \end{equation} $$ The mean value of N is given by: $$ \begin{equation} \left\langle N \right\rangle = \bra{\alpha} N \ket{\alpha} = \bra{\alpha} a^\dagger a \ket{\alpha} \end{equation} $$ So we have: $$ \begin{align} \left\langle N \right\rangle &= \bra{\alpha} a^\dagger (a \ket{\alpha}) \\ &= (\bra{\alpha} a^\dagger) \alpha \ket{\alpha} \\ &= \alpha^* \bra{\alpha} a \ket{\alpha} \text{ with } \bra{\alpha} a^\dagger = \bra{\alpha} \alpha^*\\ &= \alpha^* \alpha \braket{\alpha|\alpha} \\ &= |\alpha|^2 \end{align} $$ Mean number of quanta photon if it's an mode of the electromagnetic field. And phonon if it's a mecanical oscillator. Now let's compute $\left\langle N^2 \right\rangle$: $$ \begin{equation} \left\langle N^2 \right\rangle = \bra{\alpha} N^2 \ket{\alpha} = \bra{\alpha} a^\dagger a a^\dagger a \ket{\alpha} \end{equation} $$ We can use the commutation relation $[a, a^\dagger] = 1$ to rewrite $a a^\dagger$ as $a a^\dagger = a^\dagger a + 1$. So we have: $$ \begin{align} \left\langle N^2 \right\rangle &= \bra{\alpha} a^\dagger (a a^\dagger) a \ket{\alpha} \\ &= \bra{\alpha} a^\dagger (a^\dagger a + 1) a \ket{\alpha} \\ &= \bra{\alpha} a^\dagger a^\dagger a a \ket{\alpha} + \bra{\alpha} a^\dagger a \ket{\alpha} \\ &= \bra{\alpha} a^\dagger a^\dagger a a \ket{\alpha} + \left\langle N \right\rangle \end{align} $$ Now we can use the fact that $a \ket{\alpha} = \alpha \ket{\alpha}$ to compute $\bra{\alpha} a^\dagger a^\dagger a a \ket{\alpha}$: $$ \begin{align} \bra{\alpha} a^\dagger a^\dagger a a \ket{\alpha} &= \bra{\alpha} a^\dagger a^\dagger (a a \ket{\alpha}) \\ &= \bra{\alpha} a^\dagger a^\dagger (\alpha^2 \ket{\alpha}) \\ &= \alpha^2 \bra{\alpha} a^\dagger a^\dagger \ket{\alpha} \\ &= \alpha^2 (\bra{\alpha} a^\dagger) \alpha^* \ket{\alpha} \\ &= \alpha^2 \alpha^* \bra{\alpha} a^\dagger \ket{\alpha} \\ &= \alpha^2 \alpha^* \alpha^* \braket{\alpha|\alpha} \\ &= |\alpha|^4 \end{align} $$ So at the end we have: $$ \begin{align} \left\langle N^2 \right\rangle &= |\alpha|^4 + \left\langle N \right\rangle \\ &= |\alpha|^4 + |\alpha|^2 \end{align} $$ Now we can compute the variance $\Delta N^2$: $$ \begin{align} \Delta N^2 &= \left\langle N^2 \right\rangle - \left\langle N \right\rangle^2 \\ &= (|\alpha|^4 + |\alpha|^2) - (|\alpha|^2)^2 \\ &= |\alpha|^2 ;\\ \Delta N &= |\alpha| \end{align} $$ It's a Poissonian distribution, the mean and the variance are equal. The standard deviation is the square root of the variance, so we have $\Delta N = \sqrt{|\alpha|^2} = |\alpha|$. For exemple we can calculate the probability of having n photons in a coherent state $\ket{\alpha}$: $$ \begin{equation} P(n) = |\braket{n|\alpha}|^2 = |c_n|^2 = e^{-|\alpha|^2} \frac{|\alpha|^{2n}}{n!} \end{equation} $$ This is the Poisson distribution with mean $|\alpha|^2$.

The next calculations of the exercises are based on the fact that the coherent state $\ket{\alpha}$ is a minimum uncertainty state, which means that it saturates the Heisenberg uncertainty relation: $$ \begin{equation} \Delta X \Delta P = \frac{\hbar}{2} \end{equation} $$ We can compute the expectation values of the position and momentum operators $X$ and $P$ in the coherent state $\ket{\alpha}$: $$ \begin{align} X = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}} (a + a^\dagger) \\ P = i \sqrt{\frac{\hbar m \omega}{2}} (a - a^\dagger) \end{align} $$ To know the evolution of the expectation values of $X$ and $P$ in time, we can use the Heisenberg picture, where the operators evolve in time according to the Heisenberg equation of motion: $$ \begin{align} \frac{da}{dt} = \frac{i}{\hbar} [H, a] \\ \frac{da^\dagger}{dt} = \frac{i}{\hbar} [H, a^\dagger] \end{align} $$ where $H = \hbar \omega (a^\dagger a + \frac{1}{2})$ is the Hamiltonian of the harmonic oscillator. We can compute the commutators: $$ \begin{align} [H, a] = \hbar \omega [a^\dagger a, a] = -\hbar \omega a \\ [H, a^\dagger] = \hbar \omega [a^\dagger a, a^\dagger] = \hbar \omega a^\dagger \end{align} $$ So we have: $$ \begin{align} \frac{da}{dt} = -i \omega a \\ \frac{da^\dagger}{dt} = i \omega a^\dagger \end{align} $$ The solutions of these differential equations are: $$ \begin{align} a(t) = a(0) e^{-i \omega t} \\ a^\dagger(t) = a^\dagger(0) e^{i \omega t} \end{align} $$ Now we can compute the expectation values of $X$ and $P$ in the coherent state $\ket{\alpha}$ at time $t$: $$ \begin{align} \left\langle X \right\rangle (t) &= \bra{\alpha} X(t) \ket{\alpha} = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}} (\bra{\alpha} a(t) \ket{\alpha} + \bra{\alpha} a^\dagger(t) \ket{\alpha}) = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}} (\alpha e^{-i \omega t} + \alpha^* e^{i \omega t}) \\ &= \sqrt{\frac{2\hbar}{m\omega}} \text{Re}(\alpha e^{-i \omega t}) \\ \left\langle P \right\rangle (t) &= \bra{\alpha} P(t) \ket{\alpha} = i \sqrt{\frac{\hbar m \omega}{2}} (\bra{\alpha} a(t) \ket{\alpha} - \bra{\alpha} a^\dagger(t) \ket{\alpha}) = i \sqrt{\frac{\hbar m \omega}{2}} (\alpha e^{-i \omega t} - \alpha^* e^{i \omega t})\\ &= \sqrt{2\hbar m \omega} \text{Im}(\alpha e^{-i \omega t}) \end{align} $$ But we know that $\alpha = |\alpha| e^{i \theta}$, so we can write: $$ \begin{align} \left\langle X \right\rangle (t) &= \sqrt{\frac{2\hbar}{m\omega}} |\alpha| \cos( \omega t - \theta) \\ \left\langle P \right\rangle (t) &= - \sqrt{2\hbar m \omega} |\alpha| \sin(\omega t - \theta) \end{align} $$ It's need to satify the Ehrenfest theorem, which states that the expectation values of the position and momentum operators in a quantum state follow the classical equations of motion. In this case, we have: $$ \begin{align} \frac{d}{dt} \left\langle X \right\rangle (t) &= \frac{1}{m} \left\langle P \right\rangle (t) \\ \frac{d}{dt} \left\langle P \right\rangle (t) &= - m \omega^2 \left\langle X \right\rangle (t) \end{align} $$ Now that we have the expectation values of $X$ and $P$ in time, we can compute the variances $\Delta X^2$ and $\Delta P^2$ to show that the coherent state saturates the Heisenberg uncertainty relation. We have: $$ \begin{align} \Delta X^2 &= \left\langle X^2 \right\rangle - \left\langle X \right\rangle^2 \\ \Delta P^2 &= \left\langle P^2 \right\rangle - \left\langle P \right\rangle^2 \end{align} $$ We can compute $\left\langle X^2 \right\rangle$ and $\left\langle P^2 \right\rangle$ using the same method as before, and we find that: $$ \begin{align} \left\langle X^2 \right\rangle &= \frac{\hbar}{2m\omega} (1 + 4|\alpha|^2 \cos^2(\omega t - \theta)) \\ \left\langle P^2 \right\rangle &= \frac{\hbar m \omega}{2}(1 + 4|\alpha|^2 sin^2(\omega t - \theta)) \end{align} $$ Now compute the variances at time $t = 0$ : $$ \begin{align} \Delta X^2 &= \frac{\hbar}{2m\omega} (1 + 4|\alpha|^2 \cos^2(\omega t - \theta)) - \frac{2\hbar}{m\omega} |\alpha|^2 \cos^2(\omega t - \theta) = \frac{\hbar}{2m\omega} \\ \Delta P^2 &= \frac{\hbar m \omega}{2} (1 + 4|\alpha|^2 \sin^2(\omega t - \theta)) - 2\hbar m \omega |\alpha|^2 \sin^2(\omega t - \theta) = \frac{\hbar m \omega}{2} \end{align} $$ We can now verify the Heisenberg uncertainty relation: $$ \begin{align} \Delta X \Delta P &= \sqrt{\Delta X^2} \sqrt{\Delta P^2} = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}} \sqrt{\frac{\hbar m \omega}{2}} = \frac{\hbar}{2} \end{align} $$ This shows that the coherent state $\ket{\alpha}$ saturates the Heisenberg uncertainty relation, and is therefore a minimum uncertainty state. The expectation values of $X$ and $P$ in time follow the classical equations of motion, and the variances $\Delta X^2$ and $\Delta P^2$ are constant in time, which means that the coherent state remains localized in phase space as it evolves. This is in contrast to a generic (non-coherent) Gaussian wavepacket, which spreads out over time due to the uncertainty principle. The coherent state is therefore often referred to as the "most classical" quantum state, as it exhibits behavior that closely resembles classical physics.

Numerical Analysis (Python)

Let's now start the numerical analysis of the coherent state in Python. We will build the wavepacket from a superposition of Hermite functions, evolve it in time via spectral decomposition, and animate it to show that it stays perfectly localized. The fisrt thing to do it's to code the staionary wave function of the $\ket{n}$ states, which are given by the Hermite functions. The $n$-th stationary state $\psi_n(x)$ is given by: $$ \begin{equation} \varphi_n(x) = \left(\frac{m\omega}{\pi \hbar}\right)^{1/4} \frac{1}{\sqrt{2^n n!}} H_n\left(\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{\hbar}} x\right) e^{-\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar} x^2} \end{equation} $$ where $H_n(x)$ is the $n$-th Hermite polynomial. We can implement this in Python using the `scipy.special.hermite` function to compute the Hermite polynomials. We will also need to define the parameters of the harmonic oscillator, such as the mass $m$, the angular frequency $\omega$, and the reduced Planck constant $\hbar$. We can then define a function to compute the stationary wave function $\psi_n(x)$ for a given $n$ and $x$. We will also need to define a range of $x$ values over which to evaluate the wave functions. 

x = np.linspace(-5, 5, 1000)
m = 1.0
omega = 1.0
hbar = 1.0
alpha = 1.0
N = 10

def phi(x,n,m,omega,hbar):
    H_n = hermite(n)
    return ((1/np.sqrt(2**n * factorial(n))) * 
    (m*omega/(np.pi*hbar))**0.25 * np.exp(-m*omega*x**2/(2*hbar)) 
    * H_n(np.sqrt(m*omega/hbar)*x))
After that we can build the coherent state $\ket{\psi(x,0)}$ as a superposition of the stationary states $\ket{\varphi_n}$ with the coefficients $c_n$ that we derived analytically. We will sum over a finite number of states $N$ to approximate the coherent state. $$ \begin{equation} \ket{\psi(x,0)} \approx \sum_{n=0}^{N} c_n \ket{\varphi_n} = e^{-|\alpha|^2/2} \sum_{n=0}^{N} \frac{\alpha^n}{\sqrt{n!}} \ket{\varphi_n} \end{equation} $$ 

    def psi(x,m,N,omega,hbar, alpha):
        return (np.exp(-np.absolute(alpha)**2/2) * 
        np.sum([alpha**k / np.sqrt(factorial(k)) *
        phi(x,k,m,omega,hbar) for k in range(N)], axis=0))
For $N_{max} = 10$, we can plot the coherent state $\ket{\psi(x,0)}$ for a given value of $\alpha$. We can also compute the time evolution of the coherent state using the time-dependent Schrödinger equation. The time evolution of the coherent state is given by: $$ \begin{equation} \ket{\psi(x,t)} = e^{-i H t / \hbar} \ket{\psi(x,0)} = e^{-i \omega t / 2} \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c_n e^{-i n \omega t} \ket{\varphi_n} \end{equation} $$ 

def psi_time(x,m,omega,hbar, alpha, N ,t):
    #E_n = hbar * omega * (n + 0.5)
    return (np.exp(-np.absolute(alpha)**2/2) *
    np.sum([alpha**k / np.sqrt(factorial(k))*np.exp(-1j* hbar * 
    omega * (k + 0.5)*t/hbar) *
    phi(x,k,m,omega,hbar) for k in range(N)], axis=0))
Here, we plot the time evolution of the coherent state $\ket{\psi(x,t)}$ for different time steps $t$.

Now we would like to compare this behavior with that of a generic Gaussian wavepacket, which is not a coherent state. To do that we create a function to generate a generic Gaussian wavepacket. After we decompose it in the basis of the stationary states $\ket{\varphi_n}$ and evolve it in time using the same method as for the coherent state. 

def psi_non_coherent(x, sigma, x0=0):
    return (1/(2*np.pi*sigma**2))**0.25 * np.exp(-(x-x0)**2/(4*sigma**2))

def coefficients_projection(x, psi_initial, N, m, omega, hbar):
    coeffs = []
    for n in range(N):
        phi_n = phi(x, n, m, omega, hbar)
        c_n = np.trapezoid(np.conj(phi_n) * psi_initial, x)
        coeffs.append(c_n)
    return np.array(coeffs)

def general_evolution(x, coeffs, m, omega, hbar, t):
    result = np.zeros_like(x, dtype=complex)
    for n, c_n in enumerate(coeffs):
        E_n = hbar*omega*(n+0.5)
        result += c_n * np.exp(-1j*E_n*t/hbar) * phi(x, n, m, omega, hbar)
    return result
We can oberve that the generic Gaussian wavepacket spreads out over time, in contrast to the coherent state which remains localized.

To finished this exercise, we can compute the variances $\Delta X^2$ and $\Delta P^2$ for the generic Gaussian wavepacket and show that it does not saturate the Heisenberg uncertainty relation. 


def delta_X_numerique(x, psi_t):
    x_mean = np.trapezoid(x*np.absolute(psi_t)**2, x)
    x2_mean = np.trapezoid(x**2*np.absolute(psi_t)**2, x)
    return np.sqrt(x2_mean - x_mean**2)


def delta_P_numerique(x, psi_t, hbar):
    dx = x[1] - x[0]
    
    dpsi_dx = np.gradient(psi_t, dx)
    d2psi_dx2 = np.gradient(dpsi_dx, dx)
    
    p_mean = np.trapezoid(np.conj(psi_t) * (-1j*hbar) * dpsi_dx, x).real
    p2_mean = np.trapezoid(np.conj(psi_t) * (-hbar**2) * d2psi_dx2, x).real
    
    return np.sqrt(p2_mean - p_mean**2)

Conclusion

In this exercise we derive the physics by hand, encode it numerically, and use the numerics to check the derivation rather than just illustrate it. Building the habit of treating a physical constraint (a conservation law, a minimum-uncertainty bound, a known symmetry) as a built-in test suite for the code, rather than trusting a plot just because it renders without crashing. Physically, the coherent-state construction is also a natural stepping stone to a much wider range of problems. The ladder-operator machinery, the Poissonian number statistics, and the minimum-uncertainty wavepacket reappear almost unchanged in quantum optics (photon states, squeezed light), in the quantization of any bosonic field mode, and more directly relevant to a longer-term interest in curved-spacetime field theory, in the Bogoliubov transformations used to describe particle creation in non-inertial frames or expanding spacetimes (Unruh and Hawking radiation both lean on exactly this kind of algebra, generalized to non-trivial vacua). Having derived and verified the flat-spacetime version by hand and in code makes that generalization a matter of extending a machine already built, rather than starting from scratch.
